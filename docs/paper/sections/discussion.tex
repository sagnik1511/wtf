\section*{Discussion}\label{sec:limitations}
The world rules are deliberately simplified. Grid occupancy, directional
attacks, authored resource growth, sex labels, birth grants, inheritance and
terrain visibility are modeling choices rather than calibrated animal biology.
Paired consent poses a sparse simultaneous-action problem. Health, reward and
reproduction parameters can dominate population outcomes even with correct
learning code. Larger networks or faster predators do not by themselves
establish better prediction or realistic ecology.

Independent learning produces opponent nonstationarity and survivor-selection
bias. Inherited weights introduce an additional selection process that can
confound within-life learning. Lineage totals can favor family size; rates and
held-out tests are needed alongside the selector. Biological history resets in
new worlds, and model-generation depth is not evidence of reproductive fitness.
Mutation magnitude likewise needs ablation rather than an assumption that
small parameter noise is behaviorally small.

Engineering evidence is limited to historical local fixtures and supported
numeric/interpreter contracts. Timings do not establish portable scale or GPU
performance. Packed arrays and streamed writes reduce specific costs, while
private networks, experience and growing family history remain substantial.
Current replay bounds do not bound every experiment object, and exact restore
requires full learning state in RAM. Raw benchmark reports are local artifacts;
a public paper should archive the precise revisions, configs, platform details
and raw measurements needed to independently rerun each comparison.

Future work should first complete the controlled observations in
Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:observations}, including unsuccessful conditions and explicit
budgets. Further engineering work can examine native top-$k$ selection,
population-scale batching and repeated-run reporting costs, provided any
behavior/precision changes are evaluated separately. Biological calibration,
more realistic heredity, learned communication and alternative mating models
are research extensions rather than current validated capabilities.

\framework\ combines private individual learning, shared physical interactions,
decision-mediated paired reproduction and transferable model archives in a
CPU-oriented ecosystem simulator. Explicit ownership and RNG boundaries support
controlled experimentation, while separate replay, checkpoint and champion
formats preserve different kinds of state without coupling training to the
viewer. Historical measurements support specific runtime and storage reductions.
Claims about survival, coordination and inherited ecological competence remain
contingent on the planned held-out experiments.
